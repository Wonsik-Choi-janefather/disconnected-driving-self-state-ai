1. Research Concept and International Engagement


We propose an autonomous AI architecture that maintains an explicit model of its own equipment, available resources, accumulated damage, and unresolved conditions. When communication is lost or equipment fails, the system uses its remaining capabilities to compose alternative actions, assess their resource requirements, and authorize execution.


The central objective is to enable an autonomous system to recognize its own changing limitations and adapt to situations for which a complete response sequence has not been written in advance.


Two initial application areas are proposed:


Interstellar exploration: Equipment failure combined with loss of contact with Earth, requiring onboard recovery planning and preservation of essential spacecraft functions.


Autonomous driving: Network loss during operation, requiring continued onboard assessment, revised route planning, or a feasible minimal-risk response.




Our methodology has also been extended to computational work in synthetic biology and minimal-cell analysis. At the invitation of Dr. John I. Glass of the J. Craig Venter Institute, our joint research is scheduled for presentation at the 6th Minimal Cell Virtual Workshop on 21 October 2026, with Jeongin Choi presenting. This invitation provides an international forum for expert discussion of the methodology and opportunities for further collaboration.

The initial collaboration would define a focused simulation project covering communication loss, equipment degradation, resource shortages, and combinations of these conditions.


Proposed deliverables include:

Comparative evaluation against agreed baseline systems.
